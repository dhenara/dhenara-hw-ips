# Host and control implementation

This page describes the host/control contribution. The canonical [interfaces](../spec/interfaces.md), [host and integrity behavior](../spec/host-ecc.md) and [lifecycle](../spec/control.md) remain authoritative. The [programming guide](../programming/host-control.md) describes APB use; [owning verification](../verification/host-control.md) records reproduction and evidence limits. Combined channel wiring, PHY qualification and final throughput qualification belong to the integrated controller.

## Owners and clocks

`lpddr5_mc_apb` owns staging registers and all-channel publication in `pclk`. `lpddr5_mc_control_link` carries immutable configuration, exact acknowledgements and coherent status through bundled mailboxes to each channel. A channel validates its actual numeric profile and computes real pin, ECC, maintenance and lifecycle drain. A successful queue transfer cannot substitute for those witnesses. For a changed profile, a PREPARE ACK also attests actual FSP commit readiness for that same prepared epoch; the adapter must hold it until CH_CMD_FSP succeeds. A unanimous terminal decline preserves the previously valid image and epoch. Mixed readiness/decline, failure or timeout blocks admission because rollback is not established. The full committed host image and its epoch must reach the host before admission reopens.

`lpddr5_mc_host_register_decode` extracts regions, all sixteen QoS classes, per-port read/write limits, granule/ECC/posted modes and boot/configuration epochs from that coherent image. It neither validates a PHY nor authorizes traffic. `lpddr5_mc_host_core` owns burst validation, write buffering, transaction hazards, granule routing, read assembly and retained response storage in a common host clock domain. `lpddr5_mc_host_channel_link` crosses granules and their epochs to a channel and crosses physical/integrity results back; response exposure/handshake records travel separately.

`lpddr5_mc_axi_bridge` and its per-port endpoints own independent AXI clocks. A local ledger reserves an ID, beat count, slot/generation and complete response capacity before source acceptance. Its lifetime includes actual AXI handshakes and the host core's retirement acknowledgement. W data follows AW order, including malformed WLAST; an early WLAST never truncates the admitted count.

## Admission order and hazards

The global conflict order is established before source READY. The bridge reserves 64 exclusive admission buckets indexed by logical address bits11:6. A source may accept locally when it owns the complete conservative burst footprint and reserves its slot, generation, response capacity, sequence and acceptance-FIFO entry on that actual handshake. Invalid or unmapped requests require all 64 buckets. The admitted address map is injective and preserves its low six bits; disjoint bucket ownership excludes overlapping host bytes/granules. Physical bank and ECC check-line conflicts still belong to their channel owners. Per-port sequences cover AW and AR together, with AW before AR at a tie. The core consumes each sequence exactly; cross-port import may commute only while ownership proves the operations disjoint.

A miss retains one immutable candidate until its acknowledged grant. A conflict freezes every source that holds rights and revokes each complete source mask, including READY on the revoke-consumption edge. Accepted descriptor import, W, completion, response feedback and retirement continue while revocation waits. The coordinator keeps old rights reserved until every exact boot/configuration/token ACK arrives, all accepted transactions have handshaken and retired, and the imported-next sequence equals the frozen source watermark. It reserves new rights before sending the next grant. Tokens and sequences cannot silently wrap. Addresses separated by4KB can collide in these buckets and take this handover path; the address set is unrestricted.

An accepted AW immediately reserves its full byte-range hazard before any W arrives. Every write is completely buffered and validated before a granule can have a memory effect. Older unfinished overlaps block younger accesses; nonoverlapping transactions may execute in parallel after their order is known. The core uses stronger per-port response ordering than AXI requires: each direction returns transactions in admission order, which preserves every same-ID ordering requirement.

Aligned full-width INCR beats are coalesced into the configured 32/64-byte granule. Other admitted burst forms use their exact beat byte ranges and lane strobes. Region offsets use an invertible permutation, regions pack in ascending base order, bit six stripes two channels, and rank capacity accounts for inline ECC reservation. Invalid forms, holes, address-width/4KB crossings and unmapped bytes settle with DECERR and no effects. Malformed decoded W strobes/WLAST settle with SLVERR after the complete admitted W count. AxLOCK has ordinary effects and never produces EXOKAY.

## Completion, exposure and storage

Queue acceptance, physical completion, integrity settlement, transport transfer, AXI exposure and the actual handshake are separate events. Every completion retains tag, generation, boot epoch and configuration epoch. The core rejects mismatched/stale identities without changing transaction or credit state. A read result is assembled only after its channel reports complete physical and integrity results; the channel owns individual word/slice/split identity before that boundary.

For asynchronous response queues, `POSTED_EARLY=0` is the conservative implementation policy: the core waits for integrity as well as physical completion before placing an OKAY into the return transport, even when posted mode is selected. The accepted contract permits this added latency. Optional common-domain early eligibility requires an endpoint that arbitrates actual exposure and errors in that same domain; enabling it for an ordinary asynchronous FIFO would be unsafe.

The actual source endpoint owns BVALID exposure and BVALID/BREADY handshake records. A global abort must fence a still-unoffered return before it can become OKAY at the source. An already exposed response remains stable through backpressure; applicable later failures raise late-error evidence. Delayed feedback must not erase that evidence. The integration must connect the real global-fault assertion path; waiting for integrity does not eliminate a later ownership-loss race.

Completed channel data/error ownership can transfer into reserved host response RAM before the final host handshake. Unresolved posted MR43 epochs retain their full identities and exposure history independently of recyclable command/data slots, including both ranks concurrently. A second posted integrity update never creates another B. The host RAM and transaction dependency remain reserved to final handshake and retirement. This separation lets long AXI bursts progress beyond the finite channel queue depth.

## Reset, fault settlement and recovery

The CDC transports use asynchronous assertion, separately synchronized release, Gray-pointer FIFO controls and stable bundled mailboxes. Either endpoint reset flushes transport entries and latches a fence at both ends. Releasing reset never re-enables a link; both owners must explicitly rearm it after settlement and fresh epochs. The endpoint has ten FIFO transports, including lease commands and acknowledgements. Each owner waits for the actual transport reset-ready output in its own clock domain before issuing that pulse. A separate replica synchronizer or an assumed delay cannot prove that the transport accepted rearm. See the [constraint guidance](../../constraints/host/README.md).

A surviving AXI ledger remains outside remote/core reset. On a fault it stops new AW/AR, consumes outstanding W in order and returns every remaining R beat and one B per accepted write. Existing DECERR stays DECERR. Already exposed B/R values stay stable; later unoffered beats use the defined error path. Only an explicitly coordinated reset of that actual AXI interface may discard its ledger. Empty queues, an IRQ clear and elapsed time cannot prove settlement or retention.

The assembled host selects `EXTERNAL_ABORT_LEDGER=1`: after an abort, the common-domain core discards its transaction copies instead of waiting for feedback through the flushed transport. The surviving source ledgers own all remaining bus responses. Their combined fault-and-settlement witness is registered in the source domain before synchronization, so an earlier healthy-empty observation cannot authorize recovery. Quiesce also waits for an exact disable-generation acknowledgement from every source; each source must observe both synchronized enable stages clear, withdraw or finish its candidate, and wait for actual import of every accepted descriptor before acknowledging. The writer-local FIFO pending witness includes publication latency and synchronizes actual reader consumption; a temporarily empty read side cannot establish drain. The coordinator serializes disable tokens until all prior acknowledgements return, preventing repeated enable/disable pulses from aliasing an old token while a source clock is stopped. A stopped source clock cannot silently count as quiesced.

Healthy rearm waits for final retirement of all source ledger entries, as well as the fresh disable acknowledgement, before replacing configuration or resetting generations. Faulted transport abortion instead uses the surviving ledgers' actual response settlement because their old retirement queues were discarded.

A correlated channel failure returns SLVERR for that transaction without invalidating the active configuration or inventing global ownership loss. A final integrity error can settle a rejected operation that performed no physical write. This preserves the inline full-word rewrite path; transport, retention and ownership faults still use the independent global abort and destructive-recovery owner. A 25-family host-core iteration and the connected inline fault/rewrite/RMW iteration pass this correction, including rejection before a physical write. Final committed-source checks and broader fault coverage remain required.

Recovery requires complete host settlement, the channel's confirmed destructive reset, invalidated old tracking and fresh boot/training/profile epochs. Configuration sequence, source sequence, generation and tag exhaustion require a coordinated drained epoch transition; silent wrap is unsupported. No counter or synchronizer alone proves ownership recovery.

## Common controller coordinator

The [CK coordinator](../../rtl/integration/lpddr5_mc_coordinator.sv) implements shared command sequencing around explicit host and channel witnesses. Its input command must already have crossed coherently into CK. It rejects stale boot identities and replayed command sequences, dispatches each destination once despite independent readiness, and opens source admission only after every channel reports RUN with matching installed configuration, completed training and trusted memory. QUIESCE closes source admission before it sends the channel drain command. Unsolicited PHY management requests also close admission immediately; already accepted host work must continue through the channel DRAIN path until actual host settlement.

Recovery first waits for actual host settlement and every channel's fault state. Only then does it request destructive reset. Each reset confirmation must be observed low after that request and subsequently high; an old high level cannot authorize recovery. The sampled proof remains retained while channel command readiness stalls. After issuing each channel recovery command once, the coordinator waits for the next boot epoch and invalidated channel configurations before pulsing host recovery. Successful recovery still leaves admission closed and requires a new complete profile and training acknowledgement. There is no IRQ-clear input to this state machine.

The [controller wrapper](../../rtl/integration/lpddr5_mc.sv) now connects APB staging/publication, coherent command and host-image mailboxes, per-channel publication/status links, the coordinator and the actual AXI/channel datapath. APB and CK have separately synchronized release from the controller reset; AXI ports retain independent source clocks and resets. The complete channel status is registered together before coordinator decisions to avoid a combinational admission/settlement/drain feedback loop. Transport startup rearms each link once after its actual reset-ready witnesses. Runtime transport failures still require settlement and qualified reset/rearm; the current wrapper does not claim automatic transport recovery.

The wrapper’s directed recovery fixture has passed on an iteration source: accepted AW-without-W and AR obligations settle with errors after ownership loss, stale high reset confirmation is rejected, and fresh low/high confirmation advances the boot epoch and invalidates old profiles. Fresh PHY initialization, a current software training token and profile publication restore new AXI write/read service. Independent pin/byte checks pass on the traffic traces before and after the separately asserted recovery sequence. This representative smoke does not close the full reset/fault matrix or final controller qualification. An earlier fixture omitted the software TRAIN_TOKEN update after recovery and correctly received PSLVERR; the corrected test explicitly rejects that stale token before programming the current epoch.

The committed `PM_REQUEST`, `PD_IDLE` and `SR_IDLE` values now reach the coordinator through channel status. Policy zero disables opportunities; one selects power-down and two self-refresh, with a zero corresponding idle threshold disabling that opportunity. Idle counts CK only while host work is settled and admission is open. The coordinator closes admission before dispatch, waits for the actual channel entry or normal decline, and wakes sleeping destinations on held source demand or pending management/control work. Only the OR of AXI VALID indications crosses as a synchronized single bit per port; payload still requires the ordinary source handshake. The lifecycle defers WAKE acceptance through minimum residency, emits the complete power-down CS pulse, and performs an update before SRX. Unit policy/residency checks and connected power-down/wake and self-refresh/wake iterations pass, including independently sampled power-down CS pulse widths, update-before-SRX behavior and returned memory bytes. Final committed-source reproduction and the broader low-power/reset matrix remain pending.

Read and write urgency thresholds remain separate 16-bit age counts; direction weights retain all sixteen software bits. Performance accounting exports eight saturating 64-bit channel counters through canonical low/high CSR pairs. Payload bytes count successful physical/integrity completion transferred into reserved host storage; writes count enabled bytes. They do not count the later source AXI handshake. Commands count selected CA CKs, including pair halves; maintenance counts active service ticks. Maximum latency measures channel acceptance to host-storage transfer, and maximum wait measures scheduler residence. These maxima do not prove the conditional eligible-CK fairness bound.

PERF_SNAPSHOT waits for each selected status bridge's capture capacity, freezes one complete per-channel image and transfers it without allowing ordinary live snapshots to overwrite the held values. Retained occurrence notifications keep priority and their original identities. Destination capture/transfer may occur on different CKs, so a multi-channel request does not promise one shared capture edge. Focused bridge and coordinator tests qualify stalled capacity and exactly-once dispatch; final throughput measurement still uses validated source handshakes and independent pin observations.

The [owning coordinator fixture](../../dv/integration/coordinator_tb.sv) exercises these sequencing rules with explicit unit-test witnesses. It does not establish the complete wrapper behavior, real destructive-reset execution or end-to-end recovery to new AXI service. Those checks must use the actual host, channel and PHY-side owners rather than the fixture's driven status inputs.

## Portable implementation costs and current limits

The core reserves `2 * PORTS * DEPTH` transaction entries and 256 maximum-width beat records per entry. At two ports and depth 64, the unpruned 256-bit payload RAM is 2 MiB, with another 256 KiB for 32-bit strobes. Smaller bus widths can permit pruning; no synthesis result establishes that saving. Metadata, CDC FIFOs and response ledgers add storage. Large combinational hazard scans, mapping permutations and indexed multiport RAM access are timing/area risks requiring integration and synthesis assessment. No technology-specific PPA or clock closure is claimed.

The exclusive bucket mechanism removes the address-grant roundtrip from established disjoint traffic. Separate development fixtures measure 12.8 GB/s host payload for that path, while legal addresses 4 KiB apart that collide in the buckets measure 2.497561 GB/s under whole-source handover. The [verification page](../verification/host-control.md) defines the distinct transport and assembled-host windows and their limits. Bank/ECC conflicts and the final combined controller benchmark remain with their actual owners. No accepted address restriction avoids lease turnover, and these host measurements do not establish the final memory-controller rate.
